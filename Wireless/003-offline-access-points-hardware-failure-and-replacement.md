# Offline Access Points - Hardware Failure and Replacement

## Environment

Residential network with multiple wireless access points.

## Symptoms

* Multiple access points were offline.
* Wireless coverage was affected in the areas served by the failed APs.
* The affected APs did not return to normal operation during troubleshooting.

## Troubleshooting

* Verified that the affected access points were offline.
* Checked the APs and their network connectivity to determine whether the issue was configuration-related or hardware-related.
* Confirmed that the affected access points had failed and required replacement.
* Replaced the damaged access points.
* Integrated the replacement APs into the existing wireless network.
* Reconfigured the wireless SSID and password on the replacement equipment.
* Verified that the new APs came online and clients could connect to the wireless network.

## Root Cause

The access points had suffered hardware failure and were no longer operating normally.

## Solution

Replaced the failed access points, integrated the new units into the network, restored the existing SSID and wireless credentials, and verified client connectivity.

## Lessons Learned

* An offline AP is not always an adoption or configuration problem; hardware failure must also be considered.
* Verify basic connectivity and device status before spending excessive time on software troubleshooting.
* After replacing wireless hardware, restore the intended SSID and security settings and validate connectivity from a client device.
